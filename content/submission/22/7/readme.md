    Since 2008, artist Heather Barnett has engaged in The Physarum Experiments, an ongoing interdisciplinary project centered on a creative collaboration with Physarum polycephalum, a single-celled true slime mould. Despite lacking a brain or sensory organs, this amorphous organism exhibits primitive intelligence, problem-solving skills, and the capacity to anticipate events. Barnett views the slime mould as a nomadic information system that computes environmental complexities through chemical signaling, making continuous micro-decisions as it navigates its surroundings.


    Through time-lapse films, photographic studies, microscopic video of protoplasmic streaming, and participatory workshops, Barnett observes and influences the organism's natural behaviors. Her experiments range from testing the organism's navigation within maze replicas to hosting public events where visitors feed the slime mould diverse substances to map its preferences. Visually, the organism produces striking dendritic patterns that mirror natural branching systems seen in blood vessels, tree branches, and river deltas.


    Barnett’s work bridges art and biological science, drawing inspiration from scientific studies in cellular computation, city planning, decision-making, and collective behavior. By examining the slime mould's adaptive strategies, The Physarum Experiments explores themes of creative control, authorship, and non-human intelligence, challenging human perspectives on communication, cooperation, and complex problem-solving in living systems.
