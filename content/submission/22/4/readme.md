    The Noösphere: Sentient Living Architecture Systems project is a series of interactive sculptures and living-architecture testbeds that act as powerful public beacons and positive symbols of shared sustainable futures.


    The title invokes life on Earth as a vast organism surrounded by a “thinking skin.” Early in the past century, the Jesuit priest and geologist Pierre Teilhard de Chardin coined the term noosphere for this emerging sphere of thought. His optimistic vision imagined the Earth evolving as a whole organism, transformed through collective consciousness, mutual sympathy, and empathy.


    The series helps realize this vision within evolving physical structures. Suspended spheres contain intricate webs of distributed sensors, controls, lights, movement, and sound that respond interactively to visitors and their surroundings. Lights pulse and shimmer. Delicate fields shiver. Sounds gather, rise, and soften. Through next-generation distributed technology, each sculpture forms a responsive atmosphere that changes with every encounter. With the chorusing of massively distributed fields of small interactive components, the resulting sensation evokes a substantial kind of presence. The works suggest environments forming awareness through shared reactions and continual exchange.


    Open, inclusive qualities expressed by Noösphere evoke relationships extending far beyond human boundaries. The project’s symbolism carries messages of caring collective relationships and sustainable environments. Each installation develops within the needs and context of its host. As interactions accumulate, its relationships continue to change.


    Like a garden, each testbed requires ongoing care. This care extends through the sculptures into the institutions and communities that surround them. The works become part of a continuing process of adaptation, maintenance, learning, and renewal.


    The Noösphere series asks how experimental buildings and environments might grow, think, and care. It asks how collective consciousness might become tangible and how sympathy might enter the forms we construct. The word Noösphere embodies deep hope. That hope is carried by open relationships, continuing acts of care, and the possibility that many partial forms of awareness might gather into a shared life.
